# Code of Conduct

**Authority**: This document. It is subordinate to
[`PROJECT_GUIDELINES.md`](PROJECT_GUIDELINES.md), which binds every contributor,
and it governs conduct alone; [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) governs the
work.

**Applies to**: everyone taking part in this project, in any capacity, wherever
the project is conducted — the repository, its issues, its pull requests, its
commit messages and its code review, and any other venue in which a person is
representing Oxys-OS.

Oxys-OS presently has one contributor. This document is written anyway, and for
the same reason [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) is: a project that writes
its standards down only when they are first needed writes them down under
pressure, about a particular incident, and about a particular person. Standards
settled in advance are the only kind that can be applied evenly.

## 1. The standard

Conduct yourself so that the project remains one in which a competent person who
does not already know anybody here would be willing to do careful work.

That is the whole of it, and everything below is an elaboration of it rather than
an addition to it. Three things follow that are worth stating explicitly.

**Assume the other person is trying to get it right.** Most of what looks like
carelessness is missing context, and most of what looks like obstinacy is a
constraint you have not been told about yet. Ask before concluding.

**Say what you mean, plainly.** This project's prose is deliberately direct, and
its documents call a stale document a defect and a passing test that establishes
nothing a test not worth having. That register applies to work and does not
transfer to people. Directness costs nothing when it is aimed at a claim, an
assertion or a line of code; it is not directness when it is aimed at a person.

**Extend the good faith you would want.** A report made in good faith that turns
out to be mistaken is not misconduct. A question that reveals inexperience is not
a burden. Nobody's first contribution to a systems project is polished.

## 2. Criticism of the work is expected, and is not a breach of this document

This deserves a section of its own, because a code of conduct that is read as
forbidding blunt technical judgement makes a project worse in a way that is hard
to reverse and hard to see.

[`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md), Section 5, requires an assertion to be
paired with the silent failure it exists to catch, and calls an assertion that
merely confirms the code does what the code does an assertion that establishes
nothing. Saying so about a particular test is not an insult to whoever wrote it;
it is the practice this project runs on. Reviewing work here means being told
your reasoning does not hold, in writing, in public, and possibly at length.

The distinction that matters is between **the work and the person**, and it is
usually visible in a single clause:

| This is review | This is not |
| -------------- | ----------- |
| "This assertion passes when everything is broken; here is the case." | "You clearly did not think about this." |
| "The citation does not say what the comment says it says." | "Stop making things up." |
| "I do not follow the argument for this ordering." | "This is obviously wrong." |
| "This has been raised twice and is still unaddressed." | Raising it a third time with an audience. |

The left column may be uncomfortable to receive. It is not a breach of this
document, and treating it as one would be a way of putting technical objections
out of reach.

## 3. What is not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, whether sustained or in a single instance.
- Insults, demeaning remarks, or comments directed at a person's characteristics
  rather than at their work — including age, disability, appearance, ethnicity,
  sex, gender identity or expression, nationality, personal circumstances, race,
  religion, or sexual orientation.
- Sexual attention or advances of any kind, and sexualised language or imagery.
- Publishing another person's private information without their explicit
  permission, whether physical address, electronic address, or anything else they
  have not chosen to make public.
- Threats, intimidation, or deliberate attempts to make a person's participation
  unpleasant enough that they leave.
- Sustained disruption: derailing discussion, reopening settled decisions without
  new argument, or persisting after being asked to stop.
- Retaliation against anyone who reports a concern under this document, or who
  assists in its resolution.

The list is not exhaustive and is not intended to be. Conduct that is plainly
contrary to Section 1 is contrary to this document whether or not it appears
above; an enumeration treated as a boundary invites the argument that whatever is
not listed is permitted.

## 4. Reporting

Report to the **project owner**, who is the holder of the GitHub account that
owns this repository. Where the conduct concerns the project owner, or where a
report to them would be unwelcome for any other reason, GitHub's own reporting
mechanisms are available and are independent of this project entirely.

A report need not be formal and need not be complete. What is most useful is
what happened, where, when, and what you would like to see done; but a report
that says only that something is wrong is a report worth making.

**This routing has a defect and it is recorded rather than concealed.** A project
with one contributor cannot offer an internal reporting route that is
independent of that contributor, and a channel which is only public deters
precisely the reports most worth having. When this project has more than one
regular contributor, a private address is to be published in this section, and a
person other than the project owner is to be able to receive a report. Until
then, the sentence above about GitHub is not a formality; it is the independent
route.

## 5. What happens after a report

The project owner will acknowledge a report, establish what happened, and decide
what is to be done. Reports are held in confidence: the identity of a reporter is
not disclosed without their agreement, and the details are shared no further than
resolving the matter requires.

The responses available, in ascending order:

| Response | When |
| -------- | ---- |
| **A private correction** — what the conduct was, and why it was a problem. | A first instance, or conduct that looks unintentional. |
| **A public correction**, where the conduct was public. | The record needs correcting as well as the conduct. Left unanswered in public, conduct is taken by onlookers as tolerated. |
| **A temporary exclusion** from the project's venues. | The conduct continued after a correction, or was serious enough that a correction is not a proportionate answer. |
| **A permanent exclusion.** | A sustained pattern, or a single instance grave enough to make continued participation untenable. |

Two things are deliberately not among them. Nothing here is decided by vote,
there being no body to vote; and nothing here treats the volume or the technical
value of a person's contributions as a mitigating factor. A project that weighs
conduct against usefulness has decided in advance that its most prolific
contributor may do as they like, and everyone can see that it has.

Where a decision is made, the reasoning is recorded, so that a later comparable
case can be answered comparably.

## 6. If you think a decision was wrong

Say so, to the project owner, with the reason. A decision made by one person
about conduct is a decision made without a second opinion, and the absence of an
appeal route would make that permanent. Where the objection has substance the
decision will be revisited and the change of position recorded — by the same rule
the technical documents follow, under which a fault found is written down rather
than quietly corrected.

## 7. Why this is not the Contributor Covenant

The Contributor Covenant is the usual choice, is well drafted, and would have
been quicker to adopt. It was not adopted, for two reasons that are worth
recording so the decision is not repeatedly re-argued.

**The licensing.** The Covenant is distributed under CC BY 4.0, which requires
attribution. Every document in this repository is CC0 1.0, per
[`LICENSING.md`](LICENSING.md), and a single file under different terms with an
attribution obligation is exactly the sort of exception that is complied with at
first and forgotten later.

**The register.** This project's documents explain a decision by the failure it
prevents, and Sections 2 and 4 above are that convention applied to conduct —
the first because a code of conduct read as forbidding blunt review would damage
a project run on blunt review, the second because a reporting route with one
person at the end of it has a defect worth stating. Neither point is in the
Covenant, and neither could have been added to it without the document ceasing to
be the Covenant.

Nothing above is a criticism of that document. Where this one is silent and the
Covenant is clear, the Covenant is a reasonable guide to what is intended here.

## 8. Limitations

1. **One person administers this document, and that person is the project
   owner.** Section 4 records what follows from that and what is to change when
   the project has more contributors.
2. **There is no record of enforcement, because there has been none.** Should
   there be, the decisions and their reasoning are held privately; this document
   does not promise a public register, a report being a thing a person may make
   without wishing it published.
3. **This document governs conduct within the project's venues.** Conduct
   elsewhere is within its scope only where a person is representing Oxys-OS, or
   where it gives reason to believe that a person's participation here would be
   unsafe for somebody else.
